# Project FTM Analysis: Manufacturing Downtime & Machine Failure Analysis


## 📌 Project Overview

In large-scale manufacturing, equipment downtime directly translates to massive financial losses and supply chain bottlenecks. This project was engineered to perform a deep-dive analysis into machine failure patterns, identifying the specific "culprits" (machines and faults) that drive the majority of operational downtime.




## 🛠️ Tech Stack

- Visualization Tool: Power BI

- Data Modeling: Processed complex logs containing machine names, fault types, downtime duration, and cause-of-failure to create a unified reliability model.

- Key Visuals:

   - Funnel Chart: To visualize fault density by operational area.

   - MTBF/MTTR Matrix: To differentiate between high-frequency "nuisance" faults and low-frequency "catastrophic" failures.

   - Scatter/Trend Plot: Tracking downtime spikes by specific machine name and fault category.

 

## 📊 The Dashboard Goal

The primary objective was to move from broad floor monitoring to surgical precision in maintenance. By visualizing the relationship between specific machine faults, downtime duration, and MTTR (Mean Time To Repair), I provided the operations team with a roadmap for targeted technical interventions.




## 🔍 Key Insights & KPIs

- High-Impact Areas: Identified Grinding (461 faults) and Packing and Final (365 faults) as the primary operational bottlenecks through frequency distribution analysis.

- Fault Distribution: Leveraged a comprehensive breakdown of over 40+ unique fault types—including robotics, software, and mechanical issues—to pinpoint the most common causes of line stoppages.

- Efficiency Metrics: Integrated critical reliability KPIs such as MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures) and MTTR to identify machines that, while failing less often, took significantly longer to return to service.

- Predictive Patterns: Correlated specific faults (e.g., "axis error" or "software") with individual machine performance to prioritize spare part inventory and specialized technician training.




## 💡 Business Impact

This project transitioned the maintenance strategy from general oversight to data-driven resource allocation. By identifying that a small handful of areas (Grinding and Packing) were responsible for the majority of faults, management could redistribute technical staff and implement preventive measures where they would yield the highest return on investment.
